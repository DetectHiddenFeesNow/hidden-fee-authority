What Is Pricing Analysis?

Pricing Analysis uses artificial intelligence and optical character recognition to analyze documents and identify hidden fees, unexpected charges, and pricing inconsistencies that are not clearly disclosed. The system scans contracts, billing statements, service agreements, and financial documents to extract every fee term and compare it against industry benchmarks.

Common Hidden Fees Detected

Service Fees. Charges added to service agreements that are not clearly disclosed in marketing materials or initial quotes, often ranging from 5-20% of the total.

Administrative Surcharges. Processing and handling fees that are pure profit for the provider, typically $10-100 per transaction.

Escalation Clauses. Annual price increase provisions of 5-25% that are buried in fine print and not disclosed during the signup process.

Auto-Renewal Traps. Contract terms that automatically renew with fees charged before the customer can cancel.

How AI Pricing Analysis Detects Hidden Cost Discrepancies

AI pricing analysis examines vendor quotes, supplier proposals, and service pricing structures to identify hidden cost discrepancies that are not obvious from surface-level comparisons. The system extracts unit pricing, volume discounts, scalability terms, minimum commitments, and escalation formulas from pricing documents. It then calculates the total cost of ownership over multiple time horizons — 12 months, 24 months, and 36 months — to reveal the true long-term cost.

The AI engine specifically targets pricing structures that appear competitive on the surface but contain hidden costs that erode savings. These include minimum purchase requirements that force over-ordering, tiered pricing that resets annually rather than accumulating, volume discounts that apply only to quantities exceeding unrealistic thresholds, and escalation clauses that increase pricing faster than the market rate. The system also flags pricing that shifts costs between line items to obscure the true total.

Warning Signs in Vendor Pricing

  • Unit prices that decrease for higher volumes but require unrealistic minimum quantities
  • Pricing that breaks out "processing" or "handling" fees separately from product costs
  • Volume discounts that reset to zero at the beginning of each contract year
  • Shipping and handling charges calculated as a percentage rather than actual cost
  • Contract terms that allow the vendor to change pricing with less than 30 days notice
  • Rebate structures that require manual claiming and expire within short windows

Real-World Example

A manufacturing company received a vendor proposal for industrial supplies at what appeared to be 15% below their current pricing.
