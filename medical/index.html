Medical Billing: Understanding Healthcare Charges and Codes

July 2026

Industry Overview

Medical billing is one of the most complex consumer financial experiences, with charges that can be difficult to verify or understand. Billing codes, facility fees, separate provider charges, and items described in medical terminology can make it challenging for patients to identify billing errors or overcharges. Understanding common medical billing practices helps patients review their statements more effectively.

Common Hidden Fee Types

Duplicate Charges

Medical bills sometimes contain duplicate charges for the same service or procedure. These duplicates can be difficult to spot when billing codes and descriptions appear on different lines or pages of a multi-page statement.

Billing Code Errors

Upcoding occurs when a more expensive billing code is used than the service provided. Unbundling charges individual components of a procedure that should be billed as a single service. Both practices can significantly inflate medical costs.

Facility Fees

Hospital facility fees are charged for outpatient services provided at hospital-owned facilities. These fees can add hundreds or even thousands of dollars to routine medical services that might cost less at independent clinics.

Separate Provider Charges

Patients may receive separate bills from the hospital or facility, the attending physician, consulting specialists, anesthesiologists, radiologists, and laboratories, each with their own billing codes and pricing structures.

Consumer Tips and Best Practices

  • Review all documents carefully before signing. Read the fine print and terms and conditions sections to identify any fees that are not clearly explained.
  • Request a complete fee schedule in writing from the provider before agreeing to any service or purchase.
  • Compare pricing and fee structures across multiple providers to identify industry norms and potentially excessive charges.
  • Keep copies of all contracts, billing statements, and correspondence related to fees and charges.
  • Ask questions about any line item you do not understand. A reputable provider should be able to explain all charges clearly.
  • Consider using AI-powered document analysis tools to scan contracts, bills, and agreements for potentially hidden fees.

Regulatory Information and Consumer Protections

The Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Services (CMS) establishes billing codes and pricing guidelines. The No Surprises Act provides consumer protections against certain surprise medical bills. Consumers have the right to request itemized medical bills and to dispute billing errors through their insurance providers appeals process.

Consumer Protection Resource Consumers who believe they have been charged undisclosed fees may file complaints with the appropriate regulatory agency for their industry. The FTC accepts consumer complaints about deceptive practices at ReportFraud.ftc.gov.
