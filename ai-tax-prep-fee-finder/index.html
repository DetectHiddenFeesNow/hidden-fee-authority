What Is AI Tax Prep Fee Analysis?

AI tax prep fee analysis uses optical character recognition to extract pricing terms from CPA engagement letters, tax preparer quotes, and online filing service agreements. The system identifies base fees that exclude necessary schedules, state return add-ons that double the initial quote, audit protection upsells with high margins, per-form charges that aren't disclosed upfront, and administrative surcharges added at filing time. The system benchmarks each charge against IRS fee data and typical CPA pricing for similar return complexity.

How It Works

Step 1: Upload your tax preparer quote, CPA engagement letter, or tax filing service agreement. Step 2: AI extracts all fee components, form-level pricing, and add-on charges. Step 3: Each charge is compared against industry pricing data for similar return types and complexity. Step 4: Hidden surcharges, unbundled form fees, and inflated add-ons are flagged.

Types of Hidden Tax Prep Fees AI Detects

State Return Surcharge. Many preparers charge $50-150 per state return on top of the federal filing fee. AI flags state charges that exceed the federal fee.

Per-Form Pricing. Fee structures that charge per IRS schedule — Schedules C, D, E, and business forms each adding $25-100 to the total.

Audit Protection Add-Ons. $50-200 for services that are often basic correspondence handling with no real audit representation.

E-Filing Surcharge. Some preparers add $10-30 e-filing fees that are already included in their base pricing structure.

Amendment Fees. $100-300 for filing amended returns — a service that takes minimal additional work.

Administrative/Processing Fees. Vague charges of $25-75 added to the final bill without specific justification.

Prior Year Adjustments. Fees for reviewing prior year returns that were not initially prepared by the same firm.

Real-World Example

A small business owner in Chicago received a quote of $450 for tax preparation from a CPA. The final bill came to $895. AI analysis of the engagement letter revealed a base fee of $450 that only covered the 1040 form. Schedule C (self-employment) was $125 extra, Schedule E (rental property) was $100, the state return was $95, e-filing was $25, and there was a $75 "document processing fee" that wasn't mentioned in the initial quote. Hidden charges: $445 — nearly double the advertised price.
