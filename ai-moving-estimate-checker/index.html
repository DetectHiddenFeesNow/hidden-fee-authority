Why Moving Estimates Need Review

Consumers receive a binding or non-binding estimate from a moving company and assume the final cost will be close to the quote. However, moving contracts contain multiple fee types that can increase the price significantly. Weight estimates can be inaccurate leading to higher charges. Stair fees, long-carry distances, and bulky item surcharges may not be fully disclosed upfront. Packing material pricing often carries high markups. Fuel surcharges fluctuate with market conditions. The advertised estimate and the effective cost can differ substantially.

What the AI Checks

The AI examines each section of your moving estimate and contract for: estimated weight versus typical weight for your inventory; stair and elevator fees per floor; long-carry distance thresholds for charges from truck to door; packing material quantity and pricing; fuel surcharge percentage and calculation method; bulky item fees for pianos, safes, and exercise equipment; valuation coverage limitations; and any other charges or surcharges in the fine print.

Common Hidden Moving Charges

Weight Discrepancies. Moving companies estimate the shipment weight to calculate the price. If the certified scale weight at delivery is significantly different from the estimate, the price changes. AI identifies estimate weights that appear inconsistent with typical weights for similar household sizes.

Stair and Elevator Fees. Many contracts charge per floor of stairs or per elevator trip. These fees can add up for multi-story homes even when elevators are available. AI identifies the fee structure and calculates potential charges based on your move details.

Long-Carry Fees. When the moving truck cannot park directly in front of the residence, long-carry fees apply per foot beyond a certain distance. Some contracts set this threshold as low as 25 feet. AI identifies the threshold and fee rate.

Packing Material Markups. Boxes, tape, and packing supplies are often charged at 200-400 percent above retail prices. AI identifies the quantity of materials listed and can calculate whether the pricing is reasonable.

Fuel Surcharges. Variable surcharges tied to diesel prices are common but often persist even when fuel costs drop. AI examines how the surcharge is calculated and whether it adjusts with market changes.

Red Flags to Watch For

Weight estimates that seem low for the inventory listed. Stair fees that apply per floor without a cap. Long-carry distance thresholds under 50 feet. Packing material charges listed as an estimate without a not-to-exceed limit. Fuel surcharges calculated as a percentage of the total rather than a fixed rate. Valuation coverage that defaults to minimal liability rather than full value protection.

Common Misconceptions

Myth 1: A binding estimate means the price won't change. Binding estimates lock in the price based on specific services listed, but additional services requested at the last minute can be charged separately.

Myth 2: Moving insurance covers the full value of your belongings. Basic valuation coverage is often minimal at 60 cents per pound and full value protection is an additional cost.

Myth 3: Packing materials are included in the moving estimate. Most estimates exclude packing materials or charge them at retail prices.

Consumer Tips

Moving estimates are regulated by the FMCSA for interstate moves. Interstate movers are required to provide a written estimate and cannot charge more than 10 percent above a binding estimate without written authorization. Local moves are regulated by state laws that vary significantly. Always get estimates from at least three movers for comparison. Take inventory videos of your belongings before the movers arrive to document condition. Read the fine print regarding accessorial fees before signing.
