How Rideshare Pricing Works

Rideshare pricing uses a base fare plus distance and time charges, with surge multipliers applied during high-demand periods.

The advertised fare estimate typically excludes surge pricing, cancellation fees, wait time charges, booking fees, and airport surcharges.

AI identifies the percentage of each ride that goes to fees rather than the base fare and driver earnings.

Components of a Rideshare Fare

A typical rideshare fare breaks down into six components: base fare ($1-3), per-mile charge ($0.50-1.50), per-minute charge ($0.10-0.30), booking fee ($1-5), surge multiplier (1.0-5.0x), and tolls and surcharges.

The base fare, per-mile, and per-minute charges comprise the core transportation cost. The booking fee is a platform charge that covers insurance, background checks, and administrative costs.

Surge multipliers apply during high-demand periods and are the most volatile cost component.

How AI Analyzes Ride Receipts

AI analysis extracts each fare component from ride receipts and calculates the percentage each represents of the total.

It flags booking fees that exceed the standard $1.50-2.50 range, surge multipliers that persist during periods of typical demand, and wait time charges that begin before a reasonable grace period.

The system also aggregates data across multiple rides to identify patterns of surcharge application.

Common Hidden Fees Detected

Service Fees. Charges added to service agreements that are not clearly disclosed in marketing materials or initial quotes, often ranging from 5-20% of the total.

Administrative Surcharges. Processing and handling fees that are pure profit for the provider, typically $10-100 per transaction.

Escalation Clauses. Annual price increase provisions of 5-25% that are buried in fine print and not disclosed during the signup process.

Auto-Renewal Traps. Contract terms that automatically renew with fees charged before the customer can cancel.
