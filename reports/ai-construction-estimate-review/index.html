Common Hidden Fees Detected

Service Fees. Charges added to service agreements that are not clearly disclosed in marketing materials or initial quotes, often ranging from 5-20% of the total.

Administrative Surcharges. Processing and handling fees that are pure profit for the provider, typically $10-100 per transaction.

Escalation Clauses. Annual price increase provisions of 5-25% that are buried in fine print and not disclosed during the signup process.

Auto-Renewal Traps. Contract terms that automatically renew with fees charged before the customer can cancel.

How AI Reviews Construction Estimates

AI construction estimate review analyzes contractor bids, remodeling proposals, and renovation quotes for hidden pricing that inflates project costs by 20-50% above reasonable market rates. The system extracts every line item from the estimate — material quantities and unit prices, labor hours and rates, equipment costs, permit fees, cleanup charges, and profit margins — then benchmarks each component against regional construction cost databases that include current material prices and prevailing labor rates.

The AI specifically targets the most common sources of hidden cost in construction estimates: material markups that range from 30-200% over wholesale prices, labor rates that exceed regional prevailing wages without justification, change order language that permits unlimited scope modifications at the contractor's discretion, and mobilization-demobilization fees that are separate from the project estimate. Permit markup at 50-100% over actual government fees and cleanup charges that were not mentioned during the quoting process are also flagged.

Warning Signs in Construction Estimates

  • Material costs listed as "allowances" with no specific unit pricing
  • Labor rates quoted as a lump sum without hourly breakdown
  • Change order provisions that specify administrative fees for each modification
  • Cleanup and debris removal listed as an optional add-on rather than included
  • Permit fees that significantly exceed the actual cost charged by local government
  • Equipment rental charges that exceed readily available rental yard rates
