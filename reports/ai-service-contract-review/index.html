What Is Service Contract Review?

Service Contract Review uses artificial intelligence and optical character recognition to analyze documents and identify hidden fees, unexpected charges, and pricing inconsistencies that are not clearly disclosed. The system scans contracts, billing statements, service agreements, and financial documents to extract every fee term and compare it against industry benchmarks.

Common Hidden Fees Detected

Service Fees. Charges added to service agreements that are not clearly disclosed in marketing materials or initial quotes, often ranging from 5-20% of the total.

Administrative Surcharges. Processing and handling fees that are pure profit for the provider, typically $10-100 per transaction.

Escalation Clauses. Annual price increase provisions of 5-25% that are buried in fine print and not disclosed during the signup process.

Auto-Renewal Traps. Contract terms that automatically renew with fees charged before the customer can cancel.

How AI Reviews Service Contracts

AI service contract review scans maintenance agreements, home warranty plans, and recurring service terms for hidden fee structures that inflate costs over the life of the contract. The system extracts every fee component — annual service fees, trip charges, diagnostic fees, material markups, and escalation clauses — then calculates the true three-year cost of the service agreement. Each charge is compared against average rates for similar services in the same geographic region.

The analysis focuses on the most common service contract traps: annual escalation clauses that increase fees 5-15% per year regardless of market conditions, trip charges that apply to every service call including warranty-covered repairs, diagnostic fees that are charged even when the repair is covered under the service plan, and material markups that range from 30-200% over wholesale costs. Auto-renewal clauses are also flagged, particularly those with notice periods shorter than 30 days or automatic rate increases at renewal.

Warning Signs in Service Contracts

  • Annual percentage increases tied to CPI or a fixed rate without a cap on total increases
  • Service call fees that apply to each visit regardless of whether the repair is covered
  • Exclusions that remove coverage for common and expected repairs
  • Material markup clauses that do not specify a maximum percentage
  • Auto-renewal terms that require more than 30 days written notice to cancel
  • Contracts that lock in multi-year commitments with no early termination option

Real-World Example

A homeowner in Chicago signed a home warranty contract for $600 per year. Over three years, the annual fee increased to $690, $795, and $915 — a cumulative increase of 52%. Each service call had a $75 trip charge that was not covered under the warranty, and a $45 diagnostic fee was added on top of the trip charge. For a single furnace repair in year two, the homeowner paid $75 trip fee + $45 diagnostic fee + $250 for parts that the contractor had marked up 150% over wholesale. The AI analysis recommended switching to a flat-rate service plan with no trip charges.
