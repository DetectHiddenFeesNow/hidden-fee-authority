What Is AI Moving Fee Analysis?

AI moving fee analysis uses optical character recognition to scan moving contracts, binding estimates, and bills of lading for common hidden charges. The system identifies weight fraud — where movers overstate the shipment weight to increase the price — by comparing the estimated weight against documented inventory. It flags stair fees charged for every flight of stairs, long-carry fees for distances over a threshold that is set unreasonably low, packing material markups of 200-400% over retail, and fuel surcharges that persist even when fuel prices drop.

How It Works

Step 1: Upload your moving contract, estimate, or bill of lading. Step 2: AI extracts all fee components — weight estimates, mileage charges, packing costs, stair fees, long-carry fees, fuel surcharges, and add-on services. Step 3: Each fee is compared against FMCSA regulations and industry-standard pricing for similar moves. Step 4: Inflated charges, improper surcharges, and undisclosed fees are flagged.

Types of Hidden Moving Fees AI Detects

Weight Fraud. Movers overestimate shipment weight to increase charges. AI cross-references estimated weight against documented inventory.

Stair & Elevator Fees. Charges per flight of stairs that add $25-75 per floor. AI flags excessive stair fees for ground-floor moves.

Long-Carry Fees. Charges when the truck parks more than a certain distance from the door. AI flags unreasonably short distance thresholds.

Packing Material Markups. Boxes and packing materials charged at 200-400% above retail prices.

Fuel Surcharges. Variable charges tied to fuel prices that persist even when prices decline.

Bulky Item Fees. Extra charges for pianos, safes, exercise equipment, or large furniture not disclosed in the initial quote.

Real-World Example

A family moving from Boston to Philadelphia received a $3,200 quote for their 3-bedroom home. The final bill came to $5,100 — a 59% increase. AI analysis revealed the estimated weight of 6,500 lbs was actually 5,100 lbs on the certified scale (a $540 overcharge), stair fees were applied for a ground-floor unit ($150), long-carry fees were triggered at 25 feet — the distance from the truck to the door ($200), boxes were charged at $4.50 each vs the $1.50 retail price ($180 overcharge), and a fuel surcharge of 15% was applied despite fuel prices being below the threshold defined in the contract ($495).
