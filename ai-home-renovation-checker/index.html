What Is AI Renovation Fee Analysis?

AI renovation fee analysis uses optical character recognition to extract every line item from contractor estimates, then benchmarks each cost against regional construction pricing databases. The system identifies material costs marked up 200% or more, unnecessary change order fees, permit surcharges that exceed actual government fees, and cleanup charges that should be included in the base bid. The result is a transparent breakdown of fair market pricing versus the contractor's quoted amounts, giving homeowners specific data to negotiate.

How It Works

Step 1: Upload your contractor estimate, remodeling bid, or renovation contract in any format. Step 2: AI extracts all line-item costs, material prices, labor rates, and fee categories using OCR. Step 3: Each cost is compared against regional construction pricing databases and industry-standard markup ranges. Step 4: Inflated markups, unnecessary fees, and above-market rates are flagged with explanations. Step 5: You receive an estimate audit showing fair pricing for each line item.

Types of Hidden Renovation Fees AI Detects

Material Markup. Contractors typically charge 30-200% over wholesale on materials. AI flags markups exceeding 25% as excessive.

Change Order Fees. Administrative charges of $150-500 each for modifying the project scope. AI identifies repetitive or unnecessary change fees.

Permit Processing Surcharges. Many contractors add surcharges of 50-200% on top of actual permit costs.

Debris Removal & Cleanup. Often excluded from the base estimate and added as a separate $300-1,500 charge.

Equipment Rental. Charged at rates 50-100% above actual equipment rental market rates.

Mobilization/Demobilization. Fees for moving equipment to and from the site, often $500-2,500.

Real-World Example

A homeowner in Denver received a $28,000 estimate for a kitchen renovation. AI analysis revealed $4,200 in material markups (cabinets marked up 180%, countertops 140%), $600 in unnecessary change order fees built into the contract, a $450 permit surcharge where the actual permit cost was $120, and a $900 cleanup fee that the contractor later admitted was included in the project management line item. Total hidden charges: $6,150 — 22% of the estimate.
